# A-Dev: the discipline that gives developers superpowers

## Why this exists
Big-company results no longer require big-company budgets. With Augmented Development (A-Dev), one practitioner can orchestrate AI assistants to deliver enterprise-grade software without sacrificing security, testing, or structure. This is about democratizing capability, not replacing people.

## What A-Dev is
- **AI as the engine, human as the Architect.** The human sets constraints and quality gates; AI executes within them.
- **Atomic iterations (1–2 hours).** Tight loops avoid context bloat: define, implement, verify, and capture evidence in the same cycle.
- **Baseline rules of engagement.** Security-first, test-everything, simple/consistent structure, traceability (plan → prompt → commit), and "stop the line" when something breaks.
- **The ghost team.** Assign roles (Definitions, Blueprints, Implementation, QA). Each AI role reads the baseline before acting; you direct and align them.

## Impact
- **Quality density over vanity metrics.** A-Dev rewards value per line of code: 50 clean, tested lines beat 500 noisy ones.
- **Proof of contribution.** Every feature carries a digital thread: feature → atomic plan → prompt → commit/tests. This is auditable evidence, not promises.
- **Plan A only.** When something fails, you fix it inside the hour, learn why, and bake the lesson into the baseline so the system cannot repeat the mistake.

## Where this is going
- **HomeDir profiles.** Public evidence of work: baseline adherence, iteration success rate, quality density, and traceability for core flows (login, community, projects).
- **Weekend marathons as a delivery model.** Friday scope, Saturday iterations, Sunday hardening and demo evidence—repeatable and transparent.
- **Shared baselines and templates.** The repository ships checklists, templates, and glossary entries so others can adopt the same guardrails.
