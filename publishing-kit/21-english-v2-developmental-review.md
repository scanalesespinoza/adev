# English v2.0.0 Developmental Review

## Decision

The assembled English manuscript passes developmental review and may advance to copyediting.

This decision covers completeness, argument continuity, cultural adaptation, evidence calibration, and terminology at the conceptual level. It does not approve typography, production artifacts, release metadata, or publication.

## Reviewed Candidate

- Source canon: Spanish `v2.0.0` at `061425611c19a55faea90bdcd94bf99e5beb4894`.
- English manifest: `adevelopment-book/book-en/Book.txt`.
- Manifest coverage: 19 of 19 mapped targets.
- English manuscript size at assembly: 31,079 words.

## Narrative Continuity

The five-part argument remains coherent:

1. Human responsibility frames the use of agents.
2. Product intent and architecture define the software habitat.
3. The living baseline, atomic iteration, durable context, and verification form the operating practice.
4. Authority boundaries and shared coordination govern autonomy.
5. EventFlow and Homedir provide bounded experience before adoption, objections, and the final call to practice.

The closing action correctly returns to the promise made in the preliminary matter: choose a controlled repository, install a minimum baseline, and complete one verifiable iteration.

No chapter depends on the historical English manuscript for doctrine. The Spanish edition remains the declared source for this adaptation.

## Evidence Calibration

The review confirmed that:

- EventFlow commit counts remain distinguished from product value and human effort.
- Reported line counts are not presented as independently reproduced.
- Historical savings, return, and productivity estimates are not promoted to ADEV results.
- Homedir incidents remain attributed accounts where exact public artifacts are missing.
- The manuscript explicitly permits negative return, gradual adoption, and non-delegation.
- Agent autonomy is not presented as moral, legal, or organizational responsibility.

No new metric, testimonial, causal claim, or guarantee was introduced during adaptation.

## Cultural Adaptation

The English edition uses international professional English and retains Latin American origin only where it contributes meaning. Original Spanish article titles remain in their citations because they identify the linked works; their surrounding descriptions are in English.

The software-habitat metaphor remains intact and is explained before it carries architectural weight. The adaptation avoids literal Spanish cadence where it would sound ceremonial or ambiguous in English.

## Terminology Audit

The following canonical terms are stable across the manuscript:

- ADEV / AI Agentic Development;
- agent and agentic without implied human agency;
- architect as a responsibility-bearing role;
- living baseline;
- software habitat and blueprints;
- atomic iteration and micro-iteration;
- inner loop and outer loop;
- evidence chain;
- delivery and deployment as distinct concepts;
- authority as distinct from capability.

This review normalized a small number of hyphenated *inner-loop* and *outer-loop* compounds to the canonical forms and replaced one occurrence of *proof chain* with *evidence chain*.

The English glossary deliberately consolidates the Spanish entries *baseline viva* and *living baseline* into one English entry. This explains its one-heading difference from the source without semantic loss.

## Structural and Link Audit

- Every `Book.txt` target exists.
- Every relative manuscript link resolves.
- Chapter heading structures preserve the source coverage.
- All narrative chapters expose an outcome, and practical chapters connect to applicable assets.
- Remaining Spanish prose is limited to original-language titles in citations.

External-link availability must be checked again during production because external sources are mutable.

## Deferred to Copyedit

Copyediting should now focus on:

- sentence rhythm and repeated transitions;
- article, preposition, and compound-modifier consistency;
- capitalization of headings and technical terms;
- punctuation in lists, tables, and block quotations;
- consistency of contractions and first-person voice;
- final cross-reference and external-link review.

Copyediting must not strengthen claims, introduce doctrine, or erase declared uncertainty.

- title and version production files;
- PDF and EPUB generation;
- EPUBCheck and visual inspection;
- rights sign-off and author approval;
- creation of tag `v2.0.0-en` or a public release.
